# Summary Report: “Cloud Architect”

### Event Information

- **Event name:** Cloud Architect
- **Date:** June 20, 2026
- **Role:** Attendee
- **Scale:** Eight teams with five members each

### Event Objectives

- Create a practical academic competition focused on cloud computing and system architecture.
- Develop rapid thinking and teamwork under pressure.
- Reinforce knowledge of AWS services and architecture through competition.
- Provide opportunities for interns to meet and connect.

### Competition Format

- Two teams competed directly and answered questions of increasing difficulty.
- The team with the higher score advanced to the next round.
- If both teams had the same score, a tiebreaker question determined the winner based on response speed.
- Eight teams participated, with five members on each team.

#### Special Abilities

- **Minimum Risk** *(one use)*: A team could use this when uncertain. An incorrect answer received no penalty, while a correct answer earned half the question's points.
- **Star of Hope** *(one use)*: A team could use this when highly confident. A correct answer earned double points, while an incorrect answer incurred a double penalty.

### Question Topics

#### Core AWS Services

- **Amazon EC2:** Virtual servers in the cloud with control over the operating system and infrastructure configuration.
- **Amazon S3:** Object storage in the cloud.
- **Amazon RDS:** An AWS managed relational database service.
- **AWS Lambda:** Event-driven serverless compute without direct server management.
- **Amazon ECS:** A service for orchestrating and running containers on AWS.
- **AWS Elastic Beanstalk:** Application deployment with AWS managing much of the supporting infrastructure.

#### System Architecture

- **Microservices Architecture:** Dividing a system into independent services with focused responsibilities.
- **Event-Driven Architecture:** Publish/Subscribe, Point-to-point, and Streaming patterns.
- **Domain-Driven Design:** Designing systems around the business domain and its rules.
- **Serverless, Containers, and VMs:** Criteria for selecting the right compute model for each problem.

#### Cloud Strategy

- **Scaling:** The difference between scaling up and scaling out.
- **Cost Optimization:** Managing and removing unused resources to avoid unnecessary costs.
- **Shared Responsibility Model:** How operational responsibilities change across EC2, ECS, Fargate, and Lambda.

### Participation Activities

- I watched the teams compete, listened to each question, and considered my own answer before the official result was announced.
- By observing team discussions and strategies, I learned how participants applied AWS knowledge, answered rapidly, and collaborated under time pressure.

### Key Takeaways

#### Cloud and AWS Knowledge

- Better understood the differences and use cases of Amazon EC2, Amazon ECS, AWS Elastic Beanstalk, and AWS Lambda.
- Learned when serverless may be more suitable than a traditional server model.
- Understood how AWS costs can arise and why unused resources such as snapshots and idle instances should be cleaned up.
- Reinforced knowledge of synchronous, asynchronous, publish/subscribe, and streaming communication patterns and their trade-offs.

#### Teamwork

- Observed how teams assigned questions according to each member's strengths.
- Recognized how concise discussion and rapid agreement affected competition results.
- Learned how teams managed risk by choosing the right time to use special abilities instead of guessing without evidence.

#### Rapid Thinking

- Following questions under time pressure helped me think more quickly than theory-only study.
- I learned the value of recognizing knowledge gaps and choosing a lower-risk option when uncertain.
- I actively analyzed each question and tried to eliminate incorrect choices before hearing the official answer.

#### Networking

- Met and learned from interns in other teams with different backgrounds.
- Built additional connections within the cloud and technology community.
- Gained a clearer view of the cohort's overall knowledge level and areas for further study.

### Event Experience

Attending **Cloud Architect** was an enjoyable and valuable experience that presented cloud computing knowledge through an exciting competition. It was my first time watching a technical knowledge game show, where time pressure and a competitive atmosphere felt very different from ordinary study.

While watching the teams, I considered each question and compared my answer with the official result. This revealed knowledge gaps that I need to address, particularly in advanced AWS services. The Minimum Risk and Star of Hope abilities also showed how teams balanced confidence, points, and risk instead of merely selecting an answer.

Observing the team competition demonstrated the importance of fast, effective communication. Teams sometimes had only a few seconds to discuss and agree on an answer. Because every member had strengths in different areas, assigning the right person and coordinating effectively mattered more than individual ability alone.

### Lessons Learned

- Theory should be combined with hands-on practice; reading documentation is not enough without using the services directly.
- Questions I could not answer helped identify exactly what I should learn before participating in real projects.
- I should review AWS services and system architecture more systematically and spend more time practicing in the AWS Management Console.

> Overall, Cloud Architect was more than a competition. It clearly reflected what I already understood and what I still need to improve, making it a practical and valuable learning experience.
